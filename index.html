Monika Sanoria, PhD

Post-Doctoral Scholar • Active Matter & Biophysics

Center for Cellular and Biomolecular Machines, UC Merced

Physics of individual and collective motion, self-organization, and morphogenesis in living and active matter.

I study how cells and particles move, organize, and behave collectively in complex environments. I build computational models, grounded in statistical physics and tested against experimental data, to show how simple local interactions give rise to large-scale collective behavior. My work spans self-propelled particles that spontaneously cluster and separate, and multicellular clusters that migrate in response to chemical signals, with direct relevance to cancer metastasis. Next, I want to bring geometry and mechanics into this picture to understand how physical constraints guide tissue folding in development, and to turn that understanding into design principles for engineered tissues and biomaterials.

→ Read more about my research

  • Migration & Motility — how cells, clusters, particles, and polymers move under chemical and physical cues
  • Collective Dynamics & Self-Organization — how local interactions produce phase separation, coordination, and emergent order
  • Morphogenesis & Mechanics — how cell movement, geometry, and forces shape tissues (future direction)
